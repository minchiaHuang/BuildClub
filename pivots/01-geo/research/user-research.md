## Survey Questions
1. Now that everyone is searching on ChatGPT / Claude, are you familiar with GEO / AI search, and do your clients ask you for it?
2. To what extent do clients care about appearing in AI search? (yes → Q3, no → Q4)
3. Are you doing anything for them on it yet? What's been the hardest part?
4. Why do you think they don't care?
5. How do you check whether a client shows up in ChatGPT now, other than typing it in manually? Any favourite tool?
6. When AI recommends a competitor instead of you, how do you find out why?

## Pain Points (desk research, not yet validated by interviews)

**Measurement**
- Measuring visibility reliably is a huge challenge
- Data is inconsistent across platforms and can't be trusted
- AI results are too unstable to optimise or report on

**Diagnosis**
- Tools show the score, not the fix: you see you're missing, but not why or what to change
- Hard to understand what actually drives visibility

**Attribution and proof**
- Can't track AI customers: no search terms, no referral data; many users just Google the name or contact directly
- Can't connect visibility to traffic, conversions and revenue
- "I rank #1 on Google, but ChatGPT recommends my competitors — I can't quantify what it's costing me"
- Agencies don't know how to price, measure or prove ROI; AEO "still feels like snake oil"
- Click-based reports that agencies rely on are shrinking

**Cost and workarounds**
- AEO tools cost $400–600 per month
- People check by hand: they ask ChatGPT the same buyer questions every week and log who gets mentioned

**Other**
- No top-10 list, no clear rankings, no data dashboard for AI answers
- Block AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot) to protect content, or allow them to get discovered?

## Insights From Product Tests
These come from Recite's own test runs; they shape what agencies can be told.
- What the buyer asks (intent) decides the recommendation list far more than wording or persona
- Who a business thinks its competitors are is not who ChatGPT recommends (5 of top 20 matched)
- Being cited on your own site and on directories (e.g. Semrush agency directory, Clutch) goes with being recommended
- Local signals matter: Eventus Financial was recommended almost only for questions with a suburb name (8 of 9)
- Industry trial: finance, education and real estate questions all got answers with 0 refusals

## Evidence Gaps
- No first-hand evidence of demand or willingness to pay yet
- Meetup conversations with SEO people about pain points were not written down
- Reddit research (27 Sep) found little: 1 of 200 r/startups + SaaS posts relevant
