Covers Juliette, the VR relaxation founder, the UNSW founder, Navid, John and Daphene. Each has a summary and transcript in this folder. Daniel (20 Oct) is included where it helps. Recording dates are unknown (files added 21 Oct). All are short in-person chats at UTS Startups or events, with no speaker labels.

## Main takeaways

1. **Nobody wants AI to write or generate their content.** 6 of 7 (including Daniel) say AI-generated text, scripts or video is unengaging, "slop", "robotic", or a "turnoff". Daphene adds a performance claim: AI-scripted videos did worse. Only the UNSW power user is happy with volume AI output. This is now the strongest pattern across all interviews, Aaron included.
2. **The real product has to be in the content.** The VR founder needs their exact headset. John needs real students at real cafes. Daphene films real people. That supports "her footage only" in `pivots/02-content/docs/user-flow.md`.
3. **Ideas are not the bottleneck.** No one said "I don't know what to post". The time goes to **production after the idea**: shooting, editing (John, Daphene's outsourced editors) and polishing (Daniel, Juliette).
4. **AI-savvy founders already have the pipeline.** Navid (Claude + Figma + scheduler) and the UNSW founder (all Claude) can already produce drafts. What they lack is confidence in *what works* and the discipline to post. A content-generation wrapper offers them little.
5. **Channels split by business type.** B2B founders (Navid, the VR founder, Juliette, Daniel) lean LinkedIn, blog or newsletter, plus a little Instagram. Consumer-facing ones (John, Daphene's clients) are Instagram and video first. The current user flow assumes Instagram short video.
6. **Willingness to pay is weak.** Nobody pays for a content tool today. Money goes to **people** (interns, editors, influencers) and to **distribution** (Daniel's boosts, Navid's ads). Navid would rather hire an intern who "can prove results".

## What it means for the next step

**Recommendation: narrow from "startup founders" to founders or small teams who need to make video of a real, physical thing (a product, place or venue), and test only the "real footage → finished video" part of the flow.** John and the VR founder fit this exactly. Both named the gap (editing hours, no video skill) and both reject generated footage.

Concretely:

1. **Drop "AI writes your post" from the pitch.** Lead with "you film it (we tell you what shots), we cut it in your style, you approve". Keep the storyboard, guided filming and auto-edit steps. Cut or de-emphasise idea generation.
2. **Run a demo test with John and the VR founder this week.** Take one of their real formats (a cafe review, a headset session), give them a shot list, edit the footage, and measure the time saved against their current process (John: "a few hours" in CapCut). Ask what they'd pay against the cost of an intern's hours.
3. **Ask the money question in every interview.** It was missing from 3 of the 6. Frame it against what they already pay for: intern or editor hours, boosts, ads.
4. **Park these as separate ideas, not this product:**
   - Text and LinkedIn feedback for founders (Daniel, Juliette): a "non-sycophantic editor".
   - "Why didn't this post get views?" diagnostics (UNSW founder, Navid's "what works").
   - Agency project tracking (Daphene): a crowded market.
   - Daphene's "1-to-10" trend rule (high views from small accounts) is still worth borrowing for the trend scout step.
5. **Lead:** UNSW Reverse Pitch Night (from Navid) has many startups hiring marketing students. It's a good place to find founders with the "we need content but have no marketer" gap.
